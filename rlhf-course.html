Pegah Alizadeh Teaching

Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback

Master 2 MIASHS · Université Lumière Lyon 2 · 2026–2027

Course material: github.com/pegahani/rlhf-course (slides, labs and homework)

Overview

Modern language models are trained to follow instructions and to match human preferences. This course covers how that works, and the statistics behind it: instruction tuning, models of human comparisons, reward models, direct preference optimization, and reinforcement learning for language models. Each session combines a lecture with theory and a hands-on lab, where you train small language models yourself on a free GPU.

Format: 5 sessions of 3 hours, each with a lecture and a lab. Homework at three levels, from foundation to deep, helps you go further. The grade is based on a project of your choice, carried out in teams of 2 or 3.

Sessions

#TopicHands-on
1From base models to RLHF: language models, tokens and log-probabilities, instruction tuningTurn a base model into an assistant
2Preferences and reward models: Bradley-Terry and where it comes from, reward models for language models (architecture, outcome and process reward models, language models as judges), preference data and its biasesTrain a reward model for a language model
3Reinforcement learning for language models: the RLHF objective and KL, rejection sampling and best-of-N, policy gradients from scratch, REINFORCE and RLOO, PPO, GRPO and its variants, reasoning modelsTrain a model with GRPO from a checker alone (RL with verifiable rewards)
4Learning directly from preferences: DPO and its variants, label noise, disagreement between annotators, dueling banditsA small DPO run, project ideas
5Over-optimization, AI feedback and open problemsProject feedback

Learning objectives

  • Explain the post-training pipeline of language models, and the theory behind each step
  • Model human comparisons with Bradley-Terry and related models, and estimate them with honest uncertainty
  • Understand the policy gradient methods used for language models, from REINFORCE to PPO and GRPO
  • Derive and implement direct preference optimization, and understand its variants
  • Design, run and report a small research project, with baselines and proper statistics

Prerequisites

An introductory course in reinforcement learning, probability and statistics, and Python with NumPy. No previous experience with language models or PyTorch is needed: the first homework bridges from NumPy to PyTorch. Labs run in Google Colab or Kaggle, so a laptop and a Google or Kaggle account are enough.
